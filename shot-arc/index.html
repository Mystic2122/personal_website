  1. Mark the shot

    Step to the first frame where the ball has left the shooter's hands and click the ball.

    Best distance: the feet

    Step back to the last frame where both feet are flat on the floor, before the jump, and click the floor midway between them. A foot already in the air reads as closer to the rim. If the feet are hidden, skip this and the distance comes from the ball's flight instead.

  2. Track the ball

    The tracker looks for the moving ball and keeps the path that fits a real arc. If it gets it wrong, place the ball by hand on 6 to 10 frames spread across the flight, then track again.

Shot distance, angles and key frames appear here once the ball is tracked.

  1. Load the video

    The flight is measured against real gravity, so these must be right. Broadcast and normal phone video are 1; phone slow-mo played back at 30 fps is 4 or 8.

  2. Mark the court

    On a frame near the shot where the paint is clear, click its four corners and the rim. The app fits the camera to them and draws the court in blue so you can check it.

    Optional but worth it: 3 or 4 clicks on the 3-point line near the shooter sharpen the distance there.
